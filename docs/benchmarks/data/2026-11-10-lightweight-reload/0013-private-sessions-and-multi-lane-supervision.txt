## Context

ADR 0011 assigns local configuration coordination to Omnivox while retaining
independent speech workers and existing native ownership boundaries. The inactive
reload prototype consequently has a controller plus two participants per lane:
a native owner and a speech worker. Its records and transport fix two lanes.
Adding a third lane should not require another architectural special case.

The coordinator and native owners currently negotiate preparation and selection
of recovery records. Placing those responsibilities in one supervisor can remove
that coordination while retaining worker isolation. It changes the failure
boundary: independent owners can preserve idle speech after controller loss;
a combined supervisor becomes critical to its entire session.

Private client launches also let a working Omnivox version coexist with a version
under test. Coordinated reload does not require replacing that arrangement with
a shared daemon. Instance selection, session scope and lane identity should be
explicit before considering shared hosting.

## Decision

### Keep private instances as the default

Start a private Omnivox instance for each client by default. Initially an instance
contains one client session, managed by one supervisor process. Distinguish
instance identity, session identity, stable lane identity and worker incarnation
without creating a process for each identity. Foreground and notification are
default roles, not an exhaustive set of lanes.

Pin the instance to its selected executable and matching helper/data bundle.
Clients select the intended instance explicitly and verify build/protocol
compatibility before session enrollment. Stable and development instances can
coexist; use separate writable configuration/state where experimental changes
could affect the working instance. Retain existing asset pins and host/profile
management exclusion wherever instances use shared managed resources.

Sharing one instance between multiple clients remains a later extension. It
would need separate sessions, explicit attachment and lifetime rules, and bounded
resource allocation. A shared host would not itself share engine instances or
model residency. No shared daemon or endpoint discovery service is required for
the initial implementation.

### One supervisor owns a bounded collection of isolated lanes

The session supervisor owns native worker trees, retained recovery choices and
configuration transactions. Each lane retains its own speech worker process,
engine/helper instances, transient settings, output and cancellation. Native tree
ownership is established before worker initialization. Preserve helper process
boundaries and distribution constraints.

Bound lane membership, queues, retained records and native resources. Blocking
one lane's input/output or client replies must not prevent management progress,
cleanup or service to other lanes. Stop remains lane-scoped; reload covers the
participating lanes of its session. A worker replacement has a fresh incarnation
even when it retains the same stable lane identity.

Freeze membership for each reload. A joining lane waits for its resolution, then
starts gated on the retained committed configuration. Failure or removal requires
explicit reconciliation or retirement; it cannot silently shrink the required
acknowledgement set. Lane-local recovery remains possible while the supervisor
survives, subject to verified cleanup and the transaction's committed decision.

### Consolidate ownership while preserving the reload boundary

The supervisor prepares and retains exact per-lane recovery records itself,
removing separate owner acknowledgement rounds. Preserve immutable session
decisions, idempotent requests, worker validation, bounded admission gates and
receipts describing actual installed policy. Candidate recovery records must be
durable before Commit; the committed recovery choice must be established before
any worker resumes admission. Uncertain publication never permits assumed Abort.

Previously admitted requests keep their captured policy. Preference reload
preserves current client settings, independent synthesis and output. Report
success only after all required workers acknowledge the committed policy and the
terminal result is retained. The initial preference-only scope of ADR 0011 stays
in force; helper/output Apply remains proposed separately.

### Supervisor loss ends the session

Workers belong to the lifetime of their session supervisor. Supervisor loss ends
that session and requires retirement of all its worker/helper trees; a successor
does not reattach to surviving speech workers. Recovery must reconcile the
retained decision and confirm native cleanup before starting fresh workers from
the last conclusively committed configuration. Never reconstruct that recovery
configuration from subsequently edited files or replay interrupted speech.

Native lifetime enforcement and cleanup evidence must be qualified per platform.
A free claim, disconnected socket or saved PID is insufficient proof. Uncertain
publication, damaged history or unconfirmed cleanup keeps recovery blocked and
retains records and asset pins. No automatic recovery is advertised without the
necessary proof.

Client loss does not remove the supervisor's obligations: resolve or retain an
in-flight operation and retire the owned session without depending on client I/O.
This remains distinct from supervisor failure. The consolidated supervisor
replaces the separate owner/controller layout only for the new local session
path; existing managed validation/Apply ownership and standalone launch contracts
continue to apply to their existing paths.

### Preserve existing clients and service boundaries

Introduce the session path with explicit capability negotiation. Keep standalone
and older-client speech startup and their explicit restart path. Existing launch
paths remain available until their replacements are qualified. This decision
does not change ordinary stdio EOF behavior or the remote service's single-session,
two-lane, authentication and retirement contract under ADR 0004.

This record refines ADR 0011's reuse of existing local ownership
boundaries for the new session path. It extends ADR 0007's native ownership rules
to a supervisor responsible for multiple lanes, retaining assignment before
initialization, management exclusion, asset retention and confirmed retirement.
It does not authorize helper/output Apply, new engine boundaries or remote
management. Exact formats, migration stages and platform evidence belong in the
[delivery plan](../plans/session-configuration-reload.md#supervisor-migration)
and its linked references.

## Consequences and alternatives

One supervisor centralizes lane lifecycle, recovery selection and configuration
decisions. Adding lanes becomes a bounded membership operation. Worker admission
barriers and native cleanup still require coordination and evidence; fewer
processes alone establish no latency improvement. Per-lane model memory remains.

The cost is a larger supervisor failure boundary and changes to native ownership,
client startup and recovery qualification. Unlike the existing prototype,
controller replacement cannot preserve idle speech workers. Migration can reuse
source classification, immutable snapshots, policy slots, admission gates and
native cleanup checks; it must replace fixed-pair records and owner coordination.

Keeping a separate controller and owner per lane preserves existing launch paths
and surviving workers after controller loss, but retains distributed recovery
selection and extra participants as lanes are added. Combining all speech lanes
inside one process could simplify policy publication, but the current
[eSpeak adapter's](../../omnivox-tts/src/espeak.rs) synthesis and Stop state are
process-global. Independent lanes would require engine
isolation or a different scheduling/cancellation contract, plus broader engine
and I/O audits. A mandatory shared multi-client daemon adds attachment, lifetime,
fairness and version-selection obligations without being necessary for reload.

Creation or acceptance of this record establishes no implementation, native
platform qualification or release capability. Those remain separately tracked
in the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#host-configuration-follow-up).
